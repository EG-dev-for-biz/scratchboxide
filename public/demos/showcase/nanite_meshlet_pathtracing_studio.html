1. Continuous 1px Error (Zero B3DM Pop-in)
SUB-PIXEL CUT
Current Google 3D Tiles use B3DM bounding volume hierarchies that violently pop by 2–5 meters between LOD 14, 16, and 18 when flying down street canyons. Meshlet cluster DAG cuts evaluate projected error per-cluster (64 verts), smoothly transitioning fine architectural geometry with 0% visual popping.
2. Hi-Z Occlusion in Urban Canyons
GPU COMPUTE
In dense metropolitan districts (like Manhattan or Rome), 85%–92% of buildings are completely occluded by the immediate street facade. WebGPU compute shaders cull occluded clusters against the Hierarchical Z-Buffer depth pyramid in 0.038ms before vertex shaders even run.
3. Trillion Triangles in 512MB VRAM
PAGE STREAMING
Instead of choking browser memory with uncompressed 3D tiles across an entire borough, virtual page streaming packs 128KB cluster pages into a fixed GPU ring buffer. Skyscrapers on the distant horizon collapse to 2 clusters, while the column in front of you renders at sub-millimeter precision.
4. Progressive Path-Traced Relighting
PHYSICAL GI
Google 3D Tiles bake noon sunlight directly into textures. By treating the cluster DAG as a spatial BVH and decomposing photogrammetry into albedo, Monte Carlo path tracing recalculates authentic Rayleigh/Mie sky scatter, golden hour sun bounce, and dusk shadows.
