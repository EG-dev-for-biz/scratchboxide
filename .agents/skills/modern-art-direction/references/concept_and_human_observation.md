# Concept Before Polish: The 7 Pre-Execution Gates & Case Studies

> **Cardinal Invariant**: Resist making beautiful images before understanding the idea. Polish applied to an empty concept is merely expensive camouflage for creative failure.

---

## 1. The 7 Pre-Execution Interrogation Gates

Before generating a single AI image, staging a 3D scene, rendering a video shot, or designing a presentation slide, the art director must formulate explicit answers to these **7 Mandatory Questions**:

```
                       [ 1. WHAT ARE WE SAYING? ]
                                   │
                    [ 2. WHAT IS THE HUMAN OBSERVATION? ]
                                   │
                     [ 3. WHAT IS THE VISUAL IDEA? ]
                                   │
                [ 4. WHY IS THIS VISUAL LANGUAGE APPROPRIATE? ]
                                   │
                 [ 5. WHAT DOES THE AUDIENCE NOTICE FIRST? ]
                                   │
                 [ 6. WHAT DO THEY NOTICE SECOND? ]
                                   │
              [ 7. WHAT REMAINS DELIBERATELY UNEXPLAINED? ]
```

### The 7 Questions Detailed:

1. **What are we saying? (The Strategic Message)**
   - State the core thesis in one unadorned sentence without adjectives or jargon.
   - *Example*: "True endurance running is about daily persistence, not Olympic trophies."
2. **What is the human observation? (The Anchoring Truth)**
   - What unglamorous, funny, painful, or tender human truth underlies this? What does a human actually do when no one is watching?
   - *Example*: "Runners look ridiculous in public—stretching on park benches, spitting, waiting for crosswalks in freezing rain—yet they feel like kings."
3. **What is the visual idea? (The Metaphorical Proposition)**
   - What image captures that human truth with undeniable clarity?
   - *Example*: "Documenting the messy, unglamorous aftermath of a morning jog like a high-fashion editorial."
4. **Why is this visual language appropriate? (The Conceptual Alignment)**
   - Why this specific lens, lighting, camera height, and film stock? How do they directly reinforce the thesis?
   - *Example*: "Direct on-camera flash on 35mm film strips away corporate athletic myth-making; it treats the runner like a real person caught on the street at dawn, not a computer-generated superhero."
5. **What should the audience notice first? (The Primary Focal Point)**
   - The immediate visual anchor that captures attention in under 0.5 seconds.
   - *Example*: "The runner's bright red, sweat-slicked face smiling while leaning over their knees."
6. **What should they notice second? (The Contextual Discovery)**
   - The secondary detail that deepens the narrative and confirms the human truth.
   - *Example*: "A half-eaten cinnamon bagel resting on a municipal fire hydrant next to them."
7. **What should remain deliberately unexplained? (The Intrigue / Visual Hook)**
   - What detail, crop, or atmospheric element is withheld to invite curiosity rather than spoon-feeding?
   - *Example*: "A mysterious hand extending into the edge of the frame offering a paper towel, leaving the viewer wondering who their running partner is."

## 2. Primary Case Study Deconstructions: Jason Murray / Agencies

### Case Study 1: Claritin — "The Outsideologist Project" (Energy BBDO)
* **Client / Brand**: Claritin (Bayer Allergy)
* **The Obvious Response**: Smiling children with allergy symptoms running through sunny dandelion meadows, holding a bottle of Claritin. Generic, clinical, forgettable.
* **The Human Observation**: Children in North America spend less time outdoors than inmates at maximum-security prisons. Parents are paralyzed by screen addiction and outdoor hazards (allergies, dirt, ticks).
* **The Visual Idea**: Reframe outdoor play as serious, scientific field exploration. Don't lecture parents; certify their kids as official "Outsideologists."
* **Visual Language**:
  - *Aesthetic*: Vintage National Geographic expedition guides, tactical field badges, canvas explorer vests, specimen jars, tactile hand-drawn field notes.
  - *Lighting*: Rich, adventurous natural canopy light; golden sunlight filtering through pine needles; dust motes floating in forest air.
  - *Casting*: Real, curious kids with scraped knees, mud on their cheeks, and focused expressions—not child models posing for cereal boxes.
* **Why It Worked**: It turned an allergy medicine from a defensive chemical chore into an aspirational badge of childhood adventure.

### Case Study 2: Brooks Running — "It's Your Run" (Energy BBDO)
* **Client / Brand**: Brooks Running
* **The Obvious Response**: Hyper-athletic marathon runners sprinting across suspension bridges in misty dawn, bathed in dramatic stadium rim lights, set to pounding electronic music.
* **The Human Observation**: 95% of runners are not Olympians. They run to blow off steam, manage anxiety, or earn a Sunday donut. Elite sports marketing makes them feel inadequate and excluded.
* **The Visual Idea**: Unapologetically celebrate the chaotic, idiosyncratic, un-heroic truth of everyday running.
* **Visual Language**:
  - *Camera*: Documentary street perspective, eye-level, slightly off-axis, handheld camera micro-shake.
  - *Casting*: Wide range of body types, awkward running gates, people running in mismatched sweatpants or faded college hoodies.
  - *Moments*: A runner getting trapped at a pedestrian crossing dancing to stay warm; someone walking up a steep hill while panting; a runner stopping to pet every dog in the park.
* **Why It Worked**: Empathy replaced intimidation. The brand became the trusted ally of the real runner.

### Case Study 3: MaryRuth's Organics — Rebranding & Creative Direction (SWET STUDIO)
* **Client / Brand**: MaryRuth's Organics (Vitamins & Supplements)
* **The Obvious Response**: White clinical packaging, plastic supplement bottles, generic wellness influencers in white yoga pants smiling in clean marble kitchens.
* **The Human Observation**: Modern wellness culture is exhausting, performative, and punitive. People want daily vitality that feels gentle, kind, and integrated into messy family mornings.
* **The Visual Idea**: A warm, joyful, handcrafted domestic ecosystem where daily nourishment is a colorful celebration of life rather than a biohacking optimization regimen.
* **Visual Language**:
  - *Color Palette*: Saturated botanical pastels, rich berry reds, sunny dandelion yellows, and clean cream backdrops.
  - *Materiality*: Matte label finishes, glass dropper bottles, fresh sliced fruit dripping with juice, handwritten personal affirmations.
  - *Lighting*: Warm morning window light casting soft, gentle shadows across wooden kitchen tables.

### Case Study 4: Bandit Running Analysis (Air Interview Breakdown)
* **Subject**: Disruptor Athletic Apparel Brand
* **The Obvious Trap**: Replicating Nike's dramatic black-and-white stadium grandeur or Lululemon's pristine pastel yoga retreats.
* **The Subcultural Truth**: Running in major cities (New York, Chicago, London) is gritty, communal, and raw. It involves subways, potholes, dawn cold, car horns, and underground track clubs.
* **The Art Direction System**:
  - *Optics*: Direct on-camera flash photography that creates sharp, authentic drop shadows against brick and asphalt.
  - *Film Stock*: 35mm high-grain, slightly crushed blacks, natural street sodium-vapor and neon color spills.
  - *Typography*: Bold, utilitarian, industrial grotesk fonts evoking race bibs, subway signage, and street wheatpaste posters.
  - *Imperfection*: Real runners dripping with sweat, damp singlets sticking to skin, untied shoe laces, raw exhaustion.
