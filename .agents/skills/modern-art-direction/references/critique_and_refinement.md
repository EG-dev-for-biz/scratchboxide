# The Senior Art Director Critique Engine & Subtraction Protocol

> **Core Philosophy**: A junior art director keeps adding elements until the canvas is full. A senior art director ruthlessly removes elements until only the undeniable core idea remains.

---

## 1. The 17-Question Senior Art Director Self-Critique

After drafting any art direction proposal, visual concept, presentation layout, or generative prompt, the LLM must execute this **17-Question Self-Interrogation**:

```
                              [ THE 17 GATES ]
  1. Is this expected?                      10. Is the palette predictable?
  2. Is this generic?                       11. Does this resemble generic AI imagery?
  3. Is this derivative?                    12. Has polish replaced an idea?
  4. Is the reference doing too much work?  13. Could 1 strong decision replace 5 weak ones?
  5. Is the idea clear without explanation? 14. What could be removed right now?
  6. Does every choice support the concept? 15. What detail feels observed vs. invented?
  7. Is anything present just to look cool? 16. Where is the visual tension / surprise?
  8. Is the casting predictable?            17. What would a world-class AD challenge?
  9. Is the location predictable?
```

### Deep Dive into the 17 Questions:

1. **Is this expected?**
   - Did I take the first visual association that anyone would have? (e.g., Running = marathon runner; Luxury = marble mansion). If yes, discard and restart.
2. **Is this generic?**
   - Does this match the category wallpaper? If you swapped the brand logo with a competitor's, would anyone notice?
3. **Is this derivative?**
   - Am I copying a famous campaign or director's signature style rather than solving this brand's specific truth?
4. **Is the reference doing too much work?**
   - Am I relying on the coolness of a borrowed reference (e.g., "It's like a Wong Kar-wai movie") to disguise a lack of original conceptual thought?
5. **Is the idea understandable without explanation?**
   - If a viewer looks at this image for 1.5 seconds with zero copy, do they grasp the emotional proposition? If it requires a 300-word paragraph to make sense, the art direction has failed.
6. **Does every visual decision support the concept?**
   - Can I defend the lens focal length, the lighting angle, the actor's sock color, and the typography weight as direct consequences of the core idea?
7. **Is anything present simply because it looks cool?**
   - Look for gratuitous neon lights, unmotivated smoke, trendy acid-graphics, or lens flares. If it serves no narrative purpose, kill it immediately.
8. **Is the casting predictable?**
   - Did I cast an obvious 25-year-old fitness model with symmetrical cheekbones when the story calls for an authentic human with life experience etched into their skin?
9. **Is the location predictable?**
   - Did I choose a studio void, a sunny park, or a spotless modern loft because it was easy? What happens if we move this to a basement boiler room, an auto body shop, or a commuter train at dusk?
10. **Is the palette predictable?**
    - Am I using blue for tech, green for eco, pastel for wellness, or orange/teal for cinema? Derive the palette from physical materials and emotional counterpoint.
11. **Does this resemble generic AI imagery?**
    - Are the surfaces too smooth? Are the eyes too glassy? Is the lighting coming from everywhere at once? Introduce sensor grain, lens flaws, and physical asymmetry.
12. **Has polish replaced an idea?**
    - If you strip away the 8K resolution, the shiny 3D reflections, and the cinematic music, is there an actual human truth left standing?
13. **Could one strong decision replace five weaker decisions?**
    - Instead of three props, two models, and dynamic lighting, can a single hyper-specific object in harsh flat light tell the entire story with 10x more power?
14. **What could be removed? (The Subtraction Mandate)**
    - Identify at least one element that can be deleted right now without harming the narrative. Remove it.
15. **What detail makes this feel observed rather than invented?**
    - What is the tiny, specific, real-world quirk that proves someone actually observed human life? (e.g., A piece of Scotch tape holding someone's glasses together; a half-eaten banana on a luxury desk).
16. **Where is the tension? Where is the surprise?**
    - Where do two conflicting elements collide? Where does the visual rhythm break?
17. **What would a top-tier art director challenge here?**
    - Anticipate the harshest critique from a Cannes Lions jury chair. Address that critique before presenting the work.

## 2. The Subtraction Protocol (Step-by-Step)

Before any creative direction is finalized:

```
Pass 1: Audit all decorative layers (borders, drop shadows, gradient fills, secondary badges).
        --> Action: Strip them. If the layout breaks without decoration, the structure was weak.
Pass 2: Audit all background props and secondary actors.
        --> Action: Ask: "If this actor/prop is deleted, does the central concept collapse?"
        --> If No: Delete it.
Pass 3: Audit color palette complexity.
        --> Action: Can this 6-color palette be reduced to 2 foundation tones and 1 accent?
        --> If Yes: Restrict the palette.
Pass 4: Audit typographic variance.
        --> Action: Limit to a maximum of 2 type families and 3 size hierarchies per canvas.
```
