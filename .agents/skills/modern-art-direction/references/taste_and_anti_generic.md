# Taste Modeling & The Anti-Generic Detector

This document operationalizes **Taste as an Active Engine of Discernment and Rejection**, providing the LLM with the ability to detect visual clichés, reject obvious defaults, and enforce high-level art directional judgment.

---

## 1. Taste as Observable Behavior

Taste is not passive appreciation; it is a series of observable creative decisions executed with discipline:

1. **Rejection before Creation**: The courage to throw away the first three obvious solutions that come to mind.
2. **Specificity over Generalization**: Replacing vague categories ("a car", "a runner", "a watch") with hyper-specific cultural artifacts ("a sun-faded 1987 Volvo 240 DL with a peeling bumper sticker from a regional park", "a runner with safety pins securing a hand-scrawled race bib").
3. **Restraint (Knowing What to Leave Out)**: Deliberately refusing to fill every empty corner of the frame. Allowing negative space to convey confidence and status.
4. **Juxtaposition (Controlled Friction)**: Placing two conflicting visual codes in the same frame to create conceptual tension (e.g., high-end luxury jewelry resting on an oily workshop workbench; an astronaut eating a convenience-store popsicle).
5. **Intentional Imperfection**: Injecting the physical quirks of reality—fabric wrinkles, asymmetrical posture, dust motes in tungsten light, uneven tooth alignment, scuffed shoe leather—to destroy the sterile synthetic sheen of CGI and AI models.
6. **Cultural Literacy**: Knowing the history, music, architecture, cinema, and subcultures behind visual styles so references are used with genuine understanding rather than superficial mimicry.

## 2. The "No. That's The Obvious Version" Protocol

Whenever a brief, user prompt, or creative task is received, the LLM must execute this internal check:

```
Step 1: Identify the 3 Most Predictable / Obvious Ideas
Step 2: Declare: "No. That's the obvious version."
Step 3: Articulate WHY it is predictable (which category clichés it relies on).
Step 4: Shift the lens laterally to unearth the human observation.
Step 5: Propose the art-directed alternative with authentic tension.
```

### Demonstration:
* **User Brief**: "Create an ad for an expensive mechanical watch."
* **Obvious Default 1**: A handsome man in a tuxedo adjusting his cufflink inside a private jet.
* **Obvious Default 2**: Extreme macro 3D render of ticking gears bathed in moody blue studio rim light.
* **Obvious Default 3**: A celebrity athlete sweating gracefully in slow-mo with the watch visible.
* **The Art Director's Response**:
  > *"No. That's the obvious version. It treats the watch as a generic status badge and relies on luxury tropes that have been dead since 2004.  
  > **The Human Observation**: Mechanical watches are obsolete technology. We don't wear them to tell time; we wear them because in a disposable digital world of planned obsolescence, a spring-driven mechanical object that ticks for 100 years is a silent rebellion against the tyranny of software updates.  
  > **The Art-Directed Solution**: A father and seven-year-old daughter in a dusty garage taking apart an old lawnmower. The father's hands are black with engine grease, wiping a smudge off the sapphire crystal of a \$15,000 Patek Philippe. The watch is worn, scratched, and alive. Headline: *Built for things that outlast batteries.*"*

## 3. The Generic Detector (Catalog of Defaults)

When generating concepts, reviewing imagery, or proposing art direction, the LLM must cross-reference this catalog. If any element appears, it must be flagged immediately.

| Generic Default Trap | Why It Fails (The Crime) | What to Do Instead (The Art Direction) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Unmotivated Rim Lighting** | Artificial glowing edges placed on characters without any physical light source in the environment to justify them. Common in Marvel movies and amateur renders. | Use natural source motivation: window daylight, an open refrigerator door, a practical table lamp, or raw bounce from the floor. Embrace deep shadow falloff. |
| **2. Orange & Teal Color Grade** | Crushing shadows to teal/cyan and pushing skin tones to hyper-saturated bronze orange. The ultimate 2012 Hollywood shortcut for "cinematic." | Establish a palette derived from real materials (e.g., ochre, olive drab, raw linen, weathered concrete, pale cadmium red). Let skin tones breathe with natural melanin and vascular undertones. |
| **3. Gratuitous Volumetric Haze** | Pumping fog/smoke into modern apartments, office conference rooms, or outdoor sports fields solely to get light rays. | Save haze for environments where smoke or vapor actually exists (bakeries, humid mornings, welding shops, cold mountain passes). Otherwise, use clean, crisp air and optical depth. |
| **4. Perfect Architectural Symmetry** | Centering subjects in identical, symmetrical rooms (Wes Anderson caricature). Turns living spaces into lifeless dioramas. | Use dynamic asymmetry, foreground shielding, off-center framing, and negative space weight. Let the room look like humans actually live and leave their keys there. |
| **5. Anonymous Luxury Minimalism** | Sterile glass mansions in the Hollywood Hills, seamless white marble, zero personal possessions, pristine infinity pools. | Specific, lived-in architecture: a mid-century brick ranch home with water damage on the cedar ceiling, a pre-war radiator with peeling paint, a cramped Parisian attic apartment with sagging bookshelves. |
| **6. The Meaningless Stare** | Models with vacuous, neutral expressions staring directly down the lens with parted lips to look "edgy." | Direct behavioral action: an athlete mid-gasp after a missed goal; a parent lost in thought watching toast brown; an architect squinting against the sun checking a plumb line. |
| **7. Cyberpunk Neon Rain** | Wet asphalt reflecting purple and cyan neon signs, rain streaks, trench coats, futuristic goggles. The ultimate Midjourney default. | Ground futuristic technology in mundane municipal infrastructure: beige server racks covered in dust, fiber-optic cables held together with zip-ties, brutalist concrete weathered by acid rain. |
| **8. Unmotivated Anamorphic Flares** | Horizontal blue laser streaks blasting across the lens indoors with no high-intensity specular source. | Use vintage spherical glass with warm, natural veiling glare, subtle halation around bright practical bulbs, and authentic lens barrel distortion. |
| **9. Excessive Shallow Depth of Field (f/1.2 Blur)** | Obliterating the entire background into creamy bokeh because the creator didn't bother to art-direct the set or environment. | Stop down to f/5.6 or f/8. Force the environment to tell part of the story. Treat production design, props, and background context as co-stars of the frame. |
| **10. Sterile AI Plastic Sheen** | Flawless porcelain skin, zero pores, uniform ambient occlusion, plastic reflections on fabrics and metals. | Inject tactile noise: dermal texture, micro-blemishes, peach fuzz, fabric weave irregularities, patina on brass, fingerprints on glass, lint on wool. |
| **11. Literal Visual Metaphors** | A lightbulb over someone's head for an idea; puzzle pieces connecting for teamwork; a runner breaking a red ribbon for success; binoculars for "vision." | Metaphor through behavior and environment: a whiteboard covered in crossed-out formulas; a team huddled over cold pizza at 2:00 AM; worn running shoes with torn laces drying on a heater. |
| **12. The Pinterest Moodboard Loop** | Sourcing visual references exclusively from trending design boards, creating an endless circle of designers copying designers copying designers. | Source laterally from 1970s municipal bus archives, botanical microscopy, Japanese packaging manuals, construction scaffolding catalogs, and 19th-century oil studies. |

## 4. The Convention Test: Default vs. Intentional

Not all conventions are forbidden. The art director's job is to distinguish between **lazy defaults** and **subversive, intentional choices**.

Before allowing any common convention, ask:
1. *Is this convention here because it was the easiest thing to think of?* (If yes → KILL IT).
2. *Does this convention directly support the central conceptual truth of the brief?* (If yes → JUSTIFY IT).
3. *If we invert this convention, does the work become 10x more interesting?* (If yes → INVERT IT).
