# The Lateral Reference Methodology & Mood Board Art Direction

> **Core Axiom**: References should *create thinking*, never decorate thinking. If a mood board merely collages what already exists, it is not art direction—it is scrapbooking.

---

## 1. The 7-Tier Lateral Reference System

When conducting visual research for any brief, the art director must never execute a literal keyword search. An LLM or creative must travel across the **7 Lateral Reference Tiers** to build deep, unexpected, and resonant visual worlds.

```
                    [ 7. UNEXPECTED REFERENCES ]
                      (Botany, Ballistics, Machinery)
                                  ▲
                    [ 6. BEHAVIORAL REFERENCES ]
                      (Human Quirks, Postures, Habits)
                                  ▲
                    [ 5. MATERIAL REFERENCES ]
                      (Patina, Rust, Weave, Concrete)
                                  ▲
                    [ 4. HISTORICAL REFERENCES ]
                      (Pre-digital Archives, Movements)
                                  ▲
                    [ 3. CULTURAL REFERENCES ]
                      (Subcultures, Rituals, Street Codes)
                                  ▲
                    [ 2. ADJACENT REFERENCES ]
                      (Parallel Industries, Workwear, Lab)
                                  ▲
                    [ 1. DIRECT REFERENCES ]
                      (Baseline Category Context)
```

### The 7 Tiers Defined:

1. **Direct References (Baseline Category Context)**:
   - *Purpose*: Understand the existing category conventions so you know what to avoid or subvert.
   - *Example*: For a running shoe, look at competitor ads (Nike, On Running, Hoka) solely to map out the category clichés.
2. **Adjacent References (Parallel Craft & Industries)**:
   - *Purpose*: Borrow functional logic from fields that solve similar physical or emotional problems.
   - *Example*: For running shoes, investigate aerospace heat-shield stitching, deep-sea diving harnesses, high-altitude mountaineering crampons, or long-distance postal courier gear.
3. **Cultural References (Subcultures & Street Codes)**:
   - *Purpose*: Anchor the work in living, breathing human communities rather than corporate focus groups.
   - *Example*: Study how midnight marathoners in Tokyo tape their blisters; look at DIY zines from 1990s London pirate radio stations; study the utility vests worn by Manhattan bike messengers.
4. **Historical References (Archival & Pre-Digital Memory)**:
   - *Purpose*: Access aesthetic vocabularies created before digital cameras and Photoshop flattened the visual world.
   - *Example*: 1970s municipal transit photography; 1950s Swiss pharmacy safety packaging; 1930s WPA posters; Kodachrome 64 slides found at an estate sale.
5. **Material References (Physical Surfaces & Tactility)**:
   - *Purpose*: Ground the project in tangible substance, wear, and texture.
   - *Example*: Salt-encrusted road asphalt; oxidized copper downspouts; raw, unfinished spruce plywood; grease-stained duck canvas; yellowed drafting vellum.
6. **Behavioral References (Unconscious Human Rituals)**:
   - *Purpose*: Capture the real, awkward, idiosyncratic truths of human bodies in motion.
   - *Example*: A runner shaking out their hands at a red light; a person biting their lower lip while threading a needle; someone leaning against a subway tile wall with one foot propped up.
7. **Unexpected References (Systemic & Natural Anomalies)**:
   - *Purpose*: Introduce radical lateral metaphors that unlock completely novel visual thinking.
   - *Example*: The cross-section of a hummingbird's heart; pneumatic dispatch tubes shooting brass canisters under New York streets; time-lapse photography of tectonic plate erosion; airport luggage conveyor systems.

## 3. How to Art Direct a Mood Board in Photoshop `[EXPLICIT: Jason Murray]`

A mood board is not a mood board until it has been **art-directed**. Submitting a collection of raw screenshots with different exposures, aspect ratios, and color balances is amateurism. Follow Jason Murray's explicit 4-step workflow:

```
[ RAW DISPARATE REFERENCES ]
             │
             ▼
1. OBJECT ISOLATION ──> Strip out distracting backgrounds, watermark logos, and visual clutter.
             │
             ▼
2. COLOR HARMONIZATION ──> Normalize white/black points; unify color temperatures via adjustment layers.
             │
             ▼
3. TEXTURE OVERLAY ──> Apply a global 35mm film grain or physical scan layer across all assets.
             │
             ▼
4. EDITORIAL HIERARCHY ──> Establish hero focal point (70-20-10) with negative space pacing.
             │
             ▼
[ UNIFIED VISUAL UNIVERSE ]
```

### Technical Execution Details:
1. **Subject Isolation**: Use Photoshop Object Selection / Pen Tool to cut out key figures or props. Remove distracting backgrounds so the eye evaluates only the intended pose, texture, or lighting quality.
2. **Color Harmonization**:
   - Add a *Curves* or *Levels* adjustment layer: match the darkest black point across all images (e.g., if one reference has crushed pure blacks and another has lifted matte blacks, level them to match).
   - Add a *Color Balance* or *Selective Color* layer: tint the highlights with a uniform temperature (e.g., subtle warm tungsten or cool overcast sky) and pull discordant saturation spikes into a coherent spectrum.
3. **Texture & Grain Normalization**:
   - Place an authentic 35mm film grain plate or subtle risograph texture layer on top of all images.
   - Set blend mode to *Overlay* or *Soft Light* at 12–18% opacity. This eliminates digital resolution discrepancies between high-res and low-res references and binds the disparate sources into a tactile, printed world.
4. **Layout Architecture**:
   - Never use a predictable 3x3 uniform grid.
   - Select ONE Hero Anchor (commands 50–60% of visual weight), TWO Supporting References (answering specific questions on light and texture), and ONE Unexpected Counterpoint (creating conceptual friction).
   - Label every reference with the exact question it answers (e.g., *"Reference for harsh top-down key light only—ignore wardrobe"*).
