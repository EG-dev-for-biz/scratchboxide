# Pipeline K: the sets a text model names for what a class was seen to be (2026-11-08)

Branch `feat/k-limestone-pick` on main `24175fd4`. MAIN's open item after #190: the real height made the ESB's set picks plain, and they were wrong: its limestone took trowelled concrete and plaster grains.

## Why the pick was wrong
The run record of the ESB's material step (run 1861376c) shows the finish read and the class naming both saying limestone ("limestone cladding", "sunlit limestone facade"; the namer: "weathered Indiana limestone cladding", "sunlit buff Indiana limestone ashlar", conf 70 to 80), yet the candidates the pick by appearance was shown were, for the three stone classes: Concrete042B, Concrete030, Asphalt012, asphalt_pit_lane, Concrete041B, brushed_concrete, Asphalt013, Asphalt011, Concrete017, Concrete014 (class 0); Asphalt009, Concrete042B, ... floor_pavement (class 1); Concrete042B, concrete_pavement_02, PavingStones068, floor_pavement, PaintedPlaster015, Plaster005, Plaster006, ... Ground030 (class 2). The colour ranking (`material-sets.ts`: colour, finish, tile size) shortlists a grey limestone among concretes, asphalts and plasters; no set of the kind the reads saw ever reached the pick, so the pick chose the least wrong concrete.

## What changed
- **`pipeline-k/material-words.ts`:** a text model reads the whole facade-scale pool (2,366 sets: id, tile size, the publisher's free text and tags; about 170 k characters) once a building and names, for each class with a confident finish read (conf at least 50), up to 6 sets whose description is the material and finish the class was seen to be. Open vocabulary: the model's knowledge (a travertine is a limestone, coursed stone has joints), nothing in the code matches words. The answer is kept in the readings store under the seen words, the pool's ids and the model, so a rebuild or another benchmark side asks nothing twice.
- **`material-sets.ts`:** the named sets join the class's shortlist (measured and ranked like the rest) and are shown to the pick by appearance beside the colour ranking's 10 nearest, recoloured to the class as every candidate is; the pick still decides by the surface. Recorded on the choice: `words.seen`, `words.sets` (those shown) and `picked` (whether the pick was one of them). A failed or unsure ask leaves the colour shortlist alone, with its reason in the progress log.
- **Orchestrator:** the text ask goes through the same model chain as the finish read (no image), the brief read when the prompt is built; the twin's stage key carries `material-words/1`.
- AGENTS.md's material regions sentence names the word shortlist.

- The words alone changed nothing: with the limestone-described sets among 16 candidates, the pick by appearance still chose concretes, because at 8 cm a texel the photo patches show no grain and any flat set passes; the colour-nearest wins. Told what the class was seen to be and which candidates the library describes as that material, the pick takes them where they pass.
- The exact-match wording of the words prompt left the shaded class ("shaded limestone or dark stone surface") with no sets; naming the nearest kind gives it travertines and marbles, and the pick takes Travertine007.
- The one stone class still on concrete (21 %, "limestone panels with aluminum or mullion trim") was shown six named sets and the pick answered none: its patches hold mullion edges, and no flat stone set passes beside them. Open.
- The viewer at 6 m: before, the trowelled concrete's pits under the raking sun; after, a flat stone with faint pores and the tile's joints. The relief now draws travertine's pores, not concrete's pits; whether travertine's pores read as Indiana limestone is the user's call (the library has no dressed limestone ashlar set; its nearest are the travertines, the limestone tiles and the coursed stone walls).
- **The travertine sets draw no height.** Travertine007, Travertine009 and Concrete042B have no estimated depth in `heights.json` (their height and normal maps disagree, so the depth fit gave none), so the head build's height step found no cell deep enough and displaced nothing, and the viewer draws their normal map only: the after capture's base is flat stone with faint pores, the before's the concrete's displaced pits. Honest consequence of the pick, not a loss: a dressed limestone is flat.
- **Open, found in the after capture:** on the shaded class (Travertine007, 1.2 m tile, grain drawn at 0.85) the tile's repeat shows as a checker of light and dark squares under the raking sun: the set's normal map carries a low-frequency pattern the size of its tile, which the tiling repeats. The grain cap (`patternFit`, `energyFit`) did not catch it because that class has no photo texture to fit against. Next: cap a set's low-frequency normal content at the tile's size (a high-pass of the normal map below the facade's repeat), or rank a set's tile-scale energy against the photo's for every class.
- Costs a building: one text call of about 45 k tokens (the pool's lines) and the look-pick's call as before; both kept in the readings store. Build time unchanged within noise (the fifth build 225 s from the cached remesh).

## MAIN's second verdict (2026-11-08) and the answer
Not at the head with the checker: material regions are on by default, so it would ship to every build. Two asks, both done in this PR.

- **The tile-scale modes removed** (`pipeline-k/material-tile-filter.ts`, commit 2cf7f95a): before a set is drawn, its normal (x and y around flat, z recomputed), its roughness (around its mean) and its height map (around its mean) lose every Fourier mode of 0.5 m wavelength and longer, i.e. the modes with at most floor(tile size / 0.5 m) cycles across the tile (a 1.2 m tile: 2; a 2 m tile: 4; a tile under 0.5 m keeps its maps). Periodic, so no seam of its own; grain, pores, joints and edges are shorter and stay. In the stage key as `tile-filter/1`.
- **The glazing classes** (the second item): in the head build the two opening classes held 8.8 % of the texels and the panes 6.8 %, so at least 2.1 % of all texels (988,056) were glazing-class texels outside any pane carrying Snow005's maps (before: 1.8 %, RoadLines018's). The text model names no set at all for "window glazing" (the library holds nothing of that kind), and a class with an empty list is now drawn without the set's grain or height: its maps flat at the read finish (roughness 0.05 to 0.1), the set only a carrier. Recorded as `choice.words.nothingOfItsKind`. A failed ask (no list) keeps the old behaviour.

- **The checker's real cause, found with the window open** (`limestone/roughness_bands.png`): the fixed build's viewer capture at the same camera still showed the squares, and the breakup shader was not on these twins (no material carries its flag). The facade's planned atlases at the base band settle it: the normal map is flat, the roughness map carries the checker (texel standard deviation 50 to 60 of 255 in the head and the filtered build, main's concrete band plain). It is the travertine's own high-contrast roughness map, drawn at the set-fit regressor's grain of 0.85 and tiled at 1.2 m; within-tile structure repeats at the tile's period whatever modes a filter removes, and main's concretes hid it only because their roughness maps are plain. The regressor bypassed the photo cap (`energyFit`) entirely.
- **The fix that follows** (commit d27024f1, `capGrain`): the regressor's grain is held to the photo's texture energy over the set's on the bands both resolve (at most 1), for every class; a class with neither measured takes the others' median, else 0.5; the cap and its source are recorded on the finish (`cap.grain`, `cap.by`, `cap.regressor`). The tile-mode filter stays (it costs nothing and removes a tile's slow tilt and shade), under `tile-filter/2` in the stage key.
- **Measured, the viewer with the window open** (`limestone/limestone-fix-sheet.jpg`, `limestone/rough_*.png`): facade_lattice_10007's planned roughness at the base band, std over the stone texels: main 2.8, head 27.7, tile filter alone 22.3, the colour-only cap 22.3 (the photo's tone varies more than travertine's colour, so it held nothing; the cap now weighs the set's roughness map too), the roughness-aware cap 10.6 (the regressor's grain on the travertines 0.85 to 0.35, recorded as `cap.regressor`). ## Gotcha for anyone recapturing a build: the server serves `/models` from its asset store
The API server answers `/models/<name>.glb` from its asset store's own copy, recorded by name when the file was first seen (`desktop-app/server/app.ts`, the store sweep; the response carries `X-Scratchbox-Asset-Store: stored`). A GLB swapped on disk under an existing name is never served: two "fixed" captures in this work were the head's bytes (the viewer's roughness atlas had the head's exact numbers), and a `fetch(url, { cache: 'reload' })` returned the stored copy's size. To show a new model in the viewer, load it by its own URL (a chat build's canvas does), never by overwriting a file. Also: a page in a hidden Browser pane (the session with no window open) runs as a background page and three.js loads no textured twin there; the chat hangs the same way.
